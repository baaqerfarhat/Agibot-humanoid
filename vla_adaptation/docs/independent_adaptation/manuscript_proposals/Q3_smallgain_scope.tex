\begin{corollary}[A contraction and small-gain special case]
\label{uni:smallgain}
If $B_k\preceq B=\left[\begin{smallmatrix}\sigma&\beta\\t&q\end{smallmatrix}\right]$
and
\begin{equation}
 \sigma<1,\qquad q<1,\qquad \beta t<(1-\sigma)(1-q),
 \label{uni:smallgain-condition}
\end{equation}
then positive weights give a storage function $V_k=p_1X_k+p_2E_k$ satisfying
$V_{k+1}\le\lambda V_k+p^\top d_k$ for some $\lambda<1$.
\end{corollary}
The inequality balances attenuation within each component against their
coupling and yields a geometric disturbance bound. Its first premise,
$\sigma<1$, is a property of the execution interface in the chosen metric,
and it does not hold on every interface we evaluate. On the Panda under
OSC\_POSE, same-command replays from perturbed states give median one-step
gap ratios of $1.00$ in joint, joint--velocity and end-effector metrics
(480 perturbations, Appendix~\ref{exp:physical}): the controller sets each
goal relative to the current pose, so a displacement neither decays nor
grows. There $\sigma=1$, the corollary is empty, and the comparison bound
propagated on logged episodes grows by the healthy residual level at every
step; Theorem~\ref{uni:coupled-theorem} is the applicable statement, and the
repair evidence on that interface is the paired counts of
Section~\ref{sec:experiments}. The premise holds on absolute-target position
servos. The GR1 right-arm joints are certified first order with sampled poles
$.72$--$.77$ (held-out increment $R^2$ $.74$--$.95$), which gives
$\sigma=.77$ and $\beta=.28$ in the joint-position metric for the same-command
comparison, the predictor's DC gain matches the probed sensitivity to within
$m=.02$, and the condition reduces to a bound on the coupling from execution
deviation to observation mismatch, $\ell<(1-\sigma)(1-m)/\beta=.79$ for a
constant gain (Appendix~\ref{uniapp:gr1}). That coupling is not measured, so
we state the constants without claiming the certificate; on ALOHA only four of
fourteen joints certify.
