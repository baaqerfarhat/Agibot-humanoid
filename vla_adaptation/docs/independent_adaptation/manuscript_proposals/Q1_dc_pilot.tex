\paragraph{A causal test of the gain mismatch.}
If the rotation-$y$ shortfall is caused by the fitted-to-probed DC-gain ratio, constraining the
predictor's DC gain to the probe should move the estimate's fixed point, while model order should
not (Proposition~\ref{uniapp:firarx-prop}). We refitted the predictor by constrained ridge regression
with each corrected channel's command-tap sum pinned to its probed sensitivity, leaving all other
settings unchanged, and registered the predictions before running. The rotation-$y$ estimate then
settles at $82\%$ of the fault on Spatial and $70.5\%$ on libero\_10 (median), against $41\%$ and
$45\%$ with the unconstrained predictor; every fitted plant class had settled near the ratio
$.37$. libero\_10 moves from $0/40$ to $18/40$ (18 rescues, 0 regressions; the unconstrained predictor
reaches $15/40$ on the same scenarios, the rotation-only oracle $22/40$), and Spatial reaches $17/20$
(11 rescues, one regression). On a healthy robot the constrained predictor scores \PENDING{} against
the frozen policy's \PENDING{}, with a healthy rotation-$y$ phantom of \PENDING. These runs use the
scenarios of the principal evaluation, so they test the mechanism by intervention rather than
supplying an independent confirmation cohort.
