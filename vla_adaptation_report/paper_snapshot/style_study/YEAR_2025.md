# ICLR 2025: complete oral corpus and close reading of eight papers

**Collection is complete: 213 unique main-conference oral papers, including all three outstanding-paper winners and all three honorable mentions.** Every PDF is readable and has a SHA-256 hash, byte count, page count, and extracted text in [manifest_2025.json](manifest_2025.json). Bulk files are in [/home/fengze/online_adaptation_research/iclr_2023_2026/2025/](/home/fengze/online_adaptation_research/iclr_2023_2026/2025/). The corpus occupies 1,592,763,101 PDF bytes and contains 6,769 pages; median length is 28 pages, range 14–150, including references and appendices.

## Scope and provenance

The [official oral event catalog](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2025/events/oral) and [official static catalog](https://iclr.cc/static/virtual/data/iclr-2025-orals-posters.json) agree on 213 oral event IDs. The static data also contain poster records for those papers: selecting every row marked “Accept (Oral)” would incorrectly count 426. The collector joins each oral to its linked poster by shared UID to recover the actual OpenReview ID, then deduplicates that ID. It excludes workshop papers.

The [official award announcement](https://blog.iclr.cc/2025/04/22/announcing-the-outstanding-paper-awards-at-iclr-2025/) names six papers, all already in this oral corpus. Its initial pool of 36 candidates is not publicly enumerated there; this collection does not invent candidate or finalist labels for other papers. The announcement describes theoretical insight, practical impact, writing, and experimental rigor as considerations; this observational study cannot determine which writing choices caused an award.

OpenReview returned a 403 challenge on the PDF probe. PDFs were instead retrieved from the [official ICLR 2025 proceedings](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025), using each publication page’s Paper link. These are public **published proceedings versions**, not reconstructed submissions or review-time drafts. Explicit mappings preserve three event/publication title changes, including AlphaEdit’s Model Editing → Knowledge Editing title. Raw catalogs, publication pages, hashes, and mappings are retained in [catalog_2025.json](catalog_2025.json). Collection used at most two simultaneous requests with transient-error backoff and no repeated challenge requests.

All 213 PDFs were parsed and text-extracted. The close-reading sample below comprises all six award/honorable papers plus two orals relevant to continuous control and robustness. Their main pages 1–10 were read; appendix headings and cited locations were checked, and one selected PDF page per paper was visually inspected. Appendix proofs were not independently verified. [reading_notes_2025.json](reading_notes_2025.json) records the exact read scope, previews, source hashes, and manual citation counts. This is a writing study, not an endorsement of every scientific claim.

## Close readings

### Safety Alignment Should be Made More Than Just a Few Tokens Deep

The introduction establishes a shared failure mechanism before proposing two repairs; p2 explicitly credits earlier attacks and limits novelty to the unifying account. Table 1 (p3) introduces a controlled intervention before Figures 1–2 (p4) visualize the diagnosis. Those small plots sit beside the paragraphs interpreting them, avoiding a detached results gallery. The particularly useful writing move is on p7: a distributional diagnostic is explicitly distinguished from the safety outcome, which is then evaluated separately. Sections 3–4 connect each intervention to the earlier diagnosis, while Tables 2–4 retain utility alongside attack outcomes. Related work is compact on p10 and expanded on p17; derivations occupy Appendix F (pp24–29). The transferable lesson is to connect mechanism, intervention, and task outcome without treating an improved intermediate signal as sufficient evidence. The first main figure arrives on p4, so an award paper need not begin with a graphical teaser. [Published paper](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/file/88be023075a5a3ff3dc3b5d26623fa22-Paper-Conference.pdf).

### Learning Dynamics of LLM Finetuning

The opening narrows an expansive topic to a precise question: how one update changes other predictions. Proposition 1 on p2 is immediately followed by an MNIST example and Figure 1 on p3, giving the symbols operational meaning before extending them to language models. A prominent assumption below that figure constrains the interpretation. Figure 2 (p6) compares several algorithms through the same visual vocabulary, making a common framework visible rather than asserting it. The text distinguishes guaranteed statements from qualitative trends on p6 and acknowledges complications in the full setting. Figures 3–4 test the explanation; Figure 5 and Table 1 on p10 separate mechanistic changes from practical evaluation. Extended related work starts on p15, followed by derivations and additional settings. This structure suits a diagnostic contribution: start with a tractable example, preserve the assumption boundary, then test consequences before introducing a modest method change. [Published paper](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/file/afe1aa79e5eea7955f553c61a307273e-Paper-Conference.pdf).

### AlphaEdit: Null-Space Constrained Knowledge Editing for Language Models

The introduction names two competing objectives rather than describing prior work only by method names. Figure 1 on p2 contrasts the objectives, representation changes, and behavior; the adjacent radial summary offers headline evidence, although its density is less suitable for precise cross-method comparisons than Table 1 on p6. The method moves from a short invariance identity on p4 to implementation details and adjacent old/new update formulas on p5. That adjacency makes the claimed modification inspectable. The practical eigenvalue threshold appears in a p4 footnote, an example of an approximation that should remain close to an exact statement. Experiments begin with four research questions on p6 and later sections answer them in order. Related work, limitations, and conclusion all fit on p10; Appendix B (pp17–19) carries proofs. Useful practice: make the minimal algebraic change explicit and give each central claim a corresponding experiment. [Published paper](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/file/29c8c615b3187ee995029284702d3f43-Paper-Conference.pdf).

### Data Shapley in One Training Run

The opening distinguishes a computational obstacle from a change in the attribution quantity being defined. This prevents efficiency from obscuring a conceptual difference. Definitions and the linearity property precede the new local/global construction on pp3–4. Theorems on pp5–6 separate first- and second-order approximations, and the text explicitly identifies the first-order connection to TracIN-Ideal. Section 4.2 then explains why a direct implementation is expensive and what computation can be reused. Empirical evaluation is organized into efficiency, fidelity, and applications: Table 1 appears on p7, while the first figures arrive only on p8. The final page gives concrete limitations involving validation availability, optimizer support, and memory. Appendix A expands related work; further derivations and implementation occupy pp21–27. This is a useful counterexample to a mandatory early-figure rule: the main contribution is made clear through definitions and a short derivation chain before quantitative plots are introduced. [Published paper](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/file/20fdaf67581e6d7157376d1ed584040a-Paper-Conference.pdf).

### SAM 2: Segment Anything in Images and Videos

Figure 1 on p1 maps three named contributions—task, model, and data—to the paper’s later sections. The introduction then describes these in that order. Dense related work on pp2–3 is grouped by problem and establishes concrete limitations before the task definition. Figure 2 (p4) shows an interaction that the system must support; Figure 3 (p5) explains the architecture implementing it. Thus examples and diagrams answer different reader questions. The data section preserves provenance: annotation phases, a quality check, the validation/test annotation process, and split construction are stated in the main text. Tables 1–2 (p7) distinguish collection efficiency from the effect of additional data, and later tables separate evaluation protocols. Appendix A provides ablations, while limitations appear in Appendix C on p22. The useful pattern is to allocate one clear explanatory role to each figure and give data provenance space alongside model details. [Published paper](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/file/45c1f6a8cbf2da59ebf2c802b4f742cd-Paper-Conference.pdf).

### Faster Cascades via Speculative Decoding

The introduction compares two existing mechanisms and explains why their strengths are complementary. Table 1 on p3 is unusually instructive: it separates target distribution, deferral decision, and execution mode across methods. This prevents a shared implementation component from being mistaken for a novel decision rule. The method distinguishes an ideal rule, its implementable approximation, and a bound on that approximation. Five short algorithms on p7 share notation and reveal their common subroutine. Table 2 (p9) labels old versus new deferral rules and states two different comparison targets rather than collapsing cost and quality. The adjacent protocol paragraph explicitly limits the claim; p10 acknowledges that the theoretically motivated rule is weaker in another setting and that lower latency can require more computation. Further related work begins on p21. Transferable practice: compare variants along independently named axes and explain where a theoretical prescription loses its empirical advantage. [Published paper](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/file/6f43166f50f26e8d8f3edc5545b0749f-Paper-Conference.pdf).

### Flat Reward in Policy Parameter Space Implies Robust Reinforcement Learning

A small navigation example on p2 makes the robustness motivation tangible before the formal definitions. The related-work discussion spans parts of pp2–4 and explicitly says that the contribution concerns understanding rather than a new optimization algorithm. Figure 2 (p6) sits next to the definitions and proposition; the same page separately labels the extension to other disturbances as informal. Results progress from one-factor perturbations in Figures 3–5 to combined mass/friction heatmaps in Figure 6 (p9). Table 1 reports nominal and perturbed values with changes, preserving the reference condition. Section 5.4 explains why reward noise requires a different protocol. These are useful presentation choices for heterogeneous robot faults. They are not a substitute for statistical checking: the p7 statement about repeated seeds does not itself establish significance, and this style study has not independently validated the theorem or empirical claims. Additional settings and proofs begin on pp14–16. [Published paper](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/file/3448058d44f133839042295f79a9a958-Paper-Conference.pdf).

### Geometry of Neural Reinforcement Learning in Continuous State and Action Spaces

The introduction separates classical reachability from states attained by a restricted policy family and training process. It also discloses the tractable neural-network model before presenting the result. The background uses a concrete manifold example in Figure 1 (p4), while the method carefully distinguishes physical time from optimization time on p7. Assumptions, a boxed theorem, its interpretation, and a short proof outline appear together, leaving the full proof to Appendix G (p31). Empirical sections follow distinct questions: approximation quality, estimated dimension, a toy construction, and a practical architecture change. Figure 3 (p9) places the theoretical bound and nominal dimension directly on the same axes as estimates. Figure 5 (p10) then evaluates the application separately. The discussion names oracle-value and deterministic-dynamics limitations. The transferable practice is to keep the restricted theoretical object visible when moving to practical evidence, without presenting a theory-inspired implementation as automatically covered by the theorem. [Published paper](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/file/c3010e98dc44b6f76df7cf82b5e12c77-Paper-Conference.pdf).

This variation supports a functional approach to references: cite the foundation of a definition, the nearest competing construction, the source of a reused component, and the relevant evaluation protocol where each is discussed. Dense grouping can efficiently map a literature; it does not replace explaining the specific difference from the closest methods. The sample does not justify a minimum number of citations per paragraph or paper.

## Practical implications for the adaptation manuscript

1. **Keep the contribution axis explicit.** A comparison table can separate residual construction, estimator update, correction directions, and controller interface. This follows the useful separation of target and execution in Faster Cascades, and the direct old/new formulas in AlphaEdit.
2. **Give each figure a question.** A framework diagram explains the signal path; a condition-by-joint matrix shows heterogeneous benefits and harms; a tracking diagnostic tests a proposed mechanism. Repeating the same aggregate score in several forms adds less than those distinct roles.
3. **Preserve the gap between diagnostics and outcomes.** Improved prediction or tracking can support a mechanism without establishing task repair. Present its empirical relation to success and the unresolved cases in the main text.
4. **Put assumptions beside the result.** State which controller interface, disturbance class, operating region, and policy randomness the argument covers before the reader sees the strongest claim. Keep a proof sketch and usable equations in the main text; place long derivations and complete configurations in the appendix.
5. **Use the appendix to expand an already understandable main argument.** The selected papers keep definitions, central evidence, and important comparison choices in the main text, while distributing detailed related work, ablations, and proofs differently. No single section order or first-figure position is universal.
