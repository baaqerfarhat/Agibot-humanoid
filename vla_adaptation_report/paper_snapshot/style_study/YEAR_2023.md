# ICLR 2023: oral distinctions and nine award-paper writing profiles

## Scope, versions, and reading depth

The [official presentation catalog](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2023/events/oral) contains **248 in-person oral events**. Unlike later years, 2023 separated acceptance distinction from presentation format: both notable-top-5% and notable-top-25% papers could present orally. The [author guide](https://iclr.cc/Conferences/2023/AuthorGuide) explains this distinction. The [official static catalog](https://iclr.cc/static/virtual/data/iclr-2023-orals-posters.json) identifies 69 top-5% and 179 top-25% papers among the scheduled orals. We also include 22 top-5% papers without a scheduled oral, yielding **270 unique papers**, including all 91 top-5% papers. This supplementary inclusion prevents the older acceptance label traditionally associated with “oral” from being missed. It does not relabel those 22 as scheduled oral presentations.

The [award announcement](https://blog.iclr.cc/2023/03/21/announcing-the-iclr-2023-outstanding-paper-award-recipients/) names **four Outstanding Papers and five honorable mentions**. All nine belong to this collection. The committee describes an initial 67-paper pool, approximately 20 intermediate shortlisted papers, and nine final selected papers, but does not enumerate the larger pools. The manifest preserves the publicly named distinctions; it does not guess which other papers were candidates.

OpenReview returned a browser-verification challenge, and the separate proceedings site has no 2023 index at the URL used for 2024–2026. Public arXiv and author-hosted versions are therefore recorded as alternatives, with source URLs, identity evidence, explicit versions where available, and hashes. **They are not asserted to be byte-identical to the accepted OpenReview PDFs.** In particular, the ensemble/distillation document is a 73-page extended treatment, and the graph-biconnectivity document is a later arXiv revision. Their page layouts cannot establish the conference's submission layout. Current download completeness is reported in the master collection receipt and index rather than frozen into this narrative.

All available PDFs receive automated text and structure extraction. The nine profiles below use targeted readings of abstracts, introductions, section roadmaps, method-defining passages, main figures/tables, and selected analysis paragraphs. Representative pages were rendered for direct inspection. This is a study of writing and argument, not an independent proof or experiment audit; it does not claim that all 270 papers or all appendix proofs were closely read. Page numbers refer to the explicitly linked downloaded version.

## Four Outstanding Papers

### Universal Few-shot Learning of Dense Prediction Tasks with Visual Token Matching

[Public PDF, arXiv 2303.14969v1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.14969v1), 26 pages; OpenReview `88nT0j5jAn`.

The introduction progresses from a practical labeling burden to a more general task definition, then states two design requirements before naming the solution. Section 2 defines the problem before Section 3 explains the mechanism, making the claimed generality inspectable. Figure 1 (p4) reuses the encoder/matching/decoder notation from the preceding equations. Table 1 (p7) separates few-shot and fully supervised comparison protocols; the qualitative grid on p8 displays the different output semantics that motivate the method. The shot-scaling plots and component ablation then answer different questions from the headline table. Related work appears on p6, after the core construction, rather than being forced into a fixed second-section position. The useful pattern is **requirement → design choice → task-level evidence**: a claim such as “general” becomes persuasive through a precise common interface and visibly heterogeneous evaluations.

### Rethinking the Expressive Power of GNNs via Graph Biconnectivity

[Public PDF, arXiv 2301.09505v3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2301.09505v3), 60 pages; OpenReview `r9hNv76KoT3`. This is a later revision, dated 2024 in the PDF.

The introduction challenges the adequacy of a familiar evaluation criterion, rather than merely proposing another architecture. Figure 1 (p2) defines the alternative object visually. Table 1 (p3) maps existing families to specific capabilities and computational costs, giving the literature review a common comparison axis. Counterexamples in Figure 2 (p5) precede the constructive method; the reader sees why each desired capability needs an argument. The subsequent theoretical development distinguishes distance choices rather than hiding them behind one model name. Experiments first test the capability predicted by the theory and then evaluate practical graph tasks. The transferable structure is **criterion → limitations of existing approaches → construction → direct and downstream tests**. For the adaptation paper, this motivates an explicit distinction between estimating a disturbance and possessing the inputs needed to correct its physical effect.

### DreamFusion: Text-to-3D using 2D Diffusion

[Public PDF, arXiv 2209.14988v1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2209.14988v1), 18 pages; OpenReview `FjNys5c7VyY`. This is the retrieved preprint layout.

The opening makes a resource mismatch concrete: a powerful existing model is available in one modality, while the desired output would ordinarily require another training resource. Related work is woven into the explanation of why the nearest constructions are insufficient. Figure 1 (p2) demonstrates output capability through multiple views and geometry-related renderings, not one attractive image. Figure 2 (p4) contrasts two computational paths using small images, arrows, and parameter symbols; the adjacent equations label the gradient factors with underbraces. The reader can locate the actual modification without decoding a large text-heavy block diagram. The lesson is to pair an immediately understandable outcome with a precise mechanism, and to visually distinguish what stays frozen from what is optimized. Its large qualitative gallery is appropriate to a generative capability claim, not a template for replacing quantitative robot evaluation with selected success frames.

### Emergence of Maps in the Memories of Blind Navigation Agents

[Public PDF, arXiv 2301.13261v1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2301.13261v1), 26 pages; OpenReview `lTt4KjHSsyl`.

The abstract presents a scientific question and a chain of findings rather than claiming a new algorithm. The introduction connects a cross-disciplinary concept to a controlled artificial setting that can rule out alternative explanations. Main sections successively examine navigation, memory duration, shortcuts, decoding, and task dependence; their headings communicate findings. Figure 3 (p6) combines a rendered navigation environment, contrasting trajectories, an intervention diagram, and a small quantitative table. Crucially, the diagram shows the information transfer between agents and the absence of training influence in the reverse direction. Later figures probe representations rather than repeating navigation success. Related work and outlook appear on p9. This is a strong narrative analogue for an audit-driven adaptation paper: organize measurements as tests of successive explanations, and make the intervention precise enough that the reader can judge what it identifies.

## Five honorable mentions

### Towards Understanding Ensemble, Knowledge Distillation and Self-Distillation in Deep Learning

[Public extended PDF, arXiv 2012.09816v3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2012.09816v3), 73 pages; OpenReview `Uuf2q9TfXGA`.

The downloaded document explicitly identifies itself as the extended treatment whose abstract appears at ICLR. Its introductory argument begins with a familiar empirical benefit and asks what existing explanations fail to capture. A high-level theoretical-results subsection arrives before the detailed formal model. Figure 1 (p3) contrasts empirical behaviors that motivate the explanation; Figures 2–5 introduce the data structure through progressively more abstract examples. The theoretical exposition returns to the same structure rather than multiplying unrelated assumptions. The useful writing lesson is to provide an accessible version of the result before its formal statement and make the model assumption observable in a concrete example. The document's length, section budget, and extensive proofs are **not** evidence for how to allocate a nine-page conference submission.

### Mastering the Game of No-Press Diplomacy via Human-Regularized Reinforcement Learning and Planning

[Public PDF, arXiv 2210.05492v1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2210.05492v1), 30 pages; OpenReview `F61FwJTZhb`.

The introduction starts from the conditions under which an established method succeeds, then gives a concrete reason those conditions fail in a different interaction setting. Background and prior work define the nearest algorithm before the modification. Figure 1 (p5) uses highlighted pseudocode lines to locate the changed rule; subsequent sections separate that rule from the complete agent built around it. Tables 1–2 distinguish evaluations against agent populations from evaluations involving human players. Figure 3 (p9) pairs a performance measure with a behavior-modeling measure across training, explaining a tradeoff rather than plotting a single best score. Main text names uncertainty and comparison protocols. The transferable lesson is to keep **algorithm, integrated system, and evaluation population** separate, especially when a favorable theorem applies to a narrower object than the deployed system.

### On the duality between contrastive and non-contrastive self-supervised learning

[Public PDF, arXiv 2206.02574v3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.02574v3), 28 pages; OpenReview `kDEL91Dufpa`.

The introduction organizes the field into two familiar families, then asks whether their apparent difference is the correct explanatory axis. Related work on p2 supplies the categories needed by the algebra in Section 3. The analysis is followed by controlled interpolations between methods and a discussion of implementation differences. Figure 1 (p7) exposes a discrepancy between an expected relationship and an underperforming baseline; Table 1 (p8) makes normalization choices explicit before further comparison. Figure 2 (p9) tests one implementation difference instead of assigning all performance changes to the theory. This is directly useful for the matched-observer study: common equations should lead to carefully matched variants and tuning, while unresolved empirical differences should remain unresolved. The theorem's restricted equivalence is distinct from a statistical equivalence claim about finite task outcomes.

### Conditional Antibody Design as 3D Equivariant Graph Translation

[Public PDF, arXiv 2208.06073v6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2208.06073v6), 21 pages; OpenReview `LFHFQbjxIiP`.

The opening lists concrete modeling deficiencies that map onto later design choices. Related work precedes a task-and-notation subsection; Figure 1 (p3) teaches the domain objects before Figure 2 (p4) explains the computation. The two figures therefore serve different levels of reader knowledge. Experimental sections separate sequence/structure modeling, conditional design, and affinity-oriented evaluation. Table 1 (p7) keeps alternative protocols in separate blocks, while the qualitative examples on p8 have identifiers and quantitative annotations. The component ablation on p9 revisits the specific mechanisms promised in the introduction. The useful pattern is a one-to-one connection between **stated deficiency, implemented component, and ablation**. Its reported computational design evaluations should not be rewritten as wet-lab evidence; presentation quality includes naming what the measurement actually represents.

### Disentanglement with Biological Constraints: A Theory of Functional Cell Types

[Public PDF, arXiv 2210.01768v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2210.01768v2), 37 pages; OpenReview `9Z_GfhZnGH`.

The introduction turns several observations into one common explanatory question. Figure 1 (p2) provides geometric proof intuition before the theorem: the same simple shape is transformed across three panels as constraints are introduced. The theorem then states its assumptions, followed immediately by an intuition paragraph. The main text distinguishes the linear proof from later nonlinear experiments. Figures 2–4 (pp4–5) pair schematic representations with quantitative measurements, keeping consistent color meanings across levels of abstraction. Later task variants test when the predicted factorization changes. The lesson is to make the theorem's mechanism visually accessible without removing its scope restrictions, and to identify empirical extensions as extensions. This motivates retaining the adaptation paper's small authority and stability examples beside the conditional statements, with full derivations in the appendix.

## Cross-paper implications

There is no universal section sequence: VTM places related work after the method, Blind Navigation places it near the end, and DreamFusion integrates it into the opening argument. The shared requirement is that the nearest competing idea is explained **before the reader must evaluate the claimed difference**. Likewise, Figures 1 and 2 can be capability previews, definitions, counterexamples, proof intuition, or algorithms. Their role follows the paper's contribution.

For the current draft, the most appropriate combination is the question-driven investigation of Blind Navigation, the capability criterion of Graph Biconnectivity, the matched-implementation comparisons of the SSL duality paper, and the theorem/intuition pairing of Biological Constraints. These are editorial analogies; the adaptation paper should cite its actual VLA and adaptive-control antecedents rather than insert unrelated award papers as decorative references.
