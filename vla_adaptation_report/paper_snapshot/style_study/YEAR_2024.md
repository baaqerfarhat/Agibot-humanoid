# ICLR 2024: complete oral/award corpus and focused writing analysis

**Coverage: 86 unique main-conference papers; 86 PDFs and page-delimited texts verified; no missing PDFs.** All 5 Outstanding Paper winners and all 11 Honorable Mentions are already among those 86 orals. The corpus excludes workshop and TinyPaper awards. Collection used at most two simultaneous requests. The [manifest](manifest_2024.json) records authors, official identifiers, designation sources, actual PDF endpoints, local paths, hashes, sizes, page counts, access status, and version labels. The [collector](collect_2024.py) and [collection receipt](collection_2024.json) preserve the source and acquisition procedure.

The count is based on the 86 actual `Oral` event cards in the [official oral catalog](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2024/events/oral), corroborated by 86 `Oral` records in the [official static catalog](https://iclr.cc/static/virtual/data/iclr-2024-orals-posters.json). The latter also contains 86 corresponding poster records carrying the oral acceptance decision: filtering on decision alone would double-count. Title and details anchors likewise duplicate event links; an unrelated navigation/login poster URL explains why a broad URL match can yield an extra item. Event-card type plus OpenReview ID is the authoritative unit here.

Public OpenReview PDF/API requests returned a browser-verification 403. Every PDF was instead obtained from its title-matched [official proceedings entry](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2024), then opened with PyMuPDF and hashed. These are **public proceedings PDFs retrieved on 2026-09-08**, not asserted to be original submissions or the latest OpenReview revisions. Raw catalogs, PDFs, text, metadata, and rendered inspection pages live under `/home/fengze/online_adaptation_research/iclr_2023_2026/2024/`; bulk copyrighted documents are outside the repository.

The [awards announcement](https://blog.iclr.cc/2024/05/06/iclr-2024-outstanding-paper-awards/) reports an initial pool of 44 and an approximately 20-paper shortlist, but publishes only the five winners and eleven honorable mentions by name. No additional `public_candidate` or `public_finalist` designation has been inferred.

**[Generalization in diffusion models arises from geometry-adaptive harmonic representations](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/file/cbaf319a4712385b5ba8a414808b5713-Paper-Conference.pdf)** — Zahra Kadkhodaie; Florentin Guth; Eero Simoncelli; Stéphane Mallat. Outstanding Paper.

The narrative begins with a definition that separates variance-based generalization from accurate modeling. Figure 2 (p. 5) then places generated examples, their nearest training neighbors, and similarity histograms in one controlled comparison: the training sets are disjoint and sampling starts from the same noise. This makes the visual claim testable rather than decorative. The mechanism is developed through Jacobian bases and error scaling, with longer derivations moved to Appendix D (p. 21 onward). Crucially, Figure 5 (p. 9) tests a low-dimensional manifold on which the proposed inductive bias is suboptimal; the failure supports the scope of the interpretation. Discussion acknowledges the missing formal characterization. For our paper, the useful pattern is a definition–diagnostic–prediction–counterexample sequence: separating residual fit from physical repair should organize the narrative, and an explanatory failure belongs beside the positive result in the main text.

**[Learning Interactive Real-World Simulators](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/file/c4d66eae503694424123b93ac0fbaf17-Paper-Conference.pdf)** — Sherry Yang; Yilun Du; Seyed Ghasemipour; Jonathan Tompson; Leslie Kaelbling; Dale Schuurmans; Pieter Abbeel. Outstanding Paper.

UniSim separates a broad system overview from claim-specific demonstrations. Figure 1 (p. 2) communicates the heterogeneous training sources, while Figure 7 (p. 7) labels language plans, simulated video, and real-robot execution in distinct rows. Nearby Tables 2–3 specify that their quantitative evaluations concern simulation; the surrounding prose separately discusses real-robot examples. This distinction is especially useful for a robotics manuscript with multiple evidence sources. The model section exposes scale and compute in the main text, then delegates dataset mixture weights and implementation details to the appendix. Related work appears late, after applications. The final page gives concrete failure modes, including unseen embodiments and visually unchanged force interactions. We should borrow the evidence labeling and concrete limitations, while avoiding the inference that a photo montage establishes a multi-robot success rate or that a model diagram alone demonstrates the repair mechanism.

**[Never Train from Scratch: Fair Comparison of Long-Sequence Models Requires Data-Driven Priors](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/file/07cf32cf61224da628157b7ed0ce994a-Paper-Conference.pdf)** — Ido Amos; Jonathan Berant; Ankit Gupta. Outstanding Paper.

This paper frames its contribution as correcting a comparison protocol. Figure 1 (p. 2) shows the resulting change in the apparent architecture gap before any long method exposition. Section 3 proceeds from reproducing the historical setting to changing initialization, comparing simpler models, varying data scale, and inspecting learned kernels. Tables distinguish previously reported results, reproduced entries, unavailable results, and computationally infeasible experiments. Figure 4 (p. 8) supplies a mechanism-oriented diagnostic after the performance comparison; compute-matched controls are discussed in the main text and developed in Appendix D. Notably, the dedicated related-work section and conclusion are placed after references on p. 14. The lesson for our observer study is to make calibrated, tuned baselines part of the contribution: a carefully established narrowing of the method gap can be scientifically valuable without manufacturing a new estimator's superiority.

**[Protein Discovery with Discrete Walk-Jump Sampling](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/file/78b6beab44f92adc74ac1fdb212ac3a0-Paper-Conference.pdf)** — Nathan Frey; Dan Berenberg; Karina Zadorozhny; Joseph Kleinhenz; Julien Lafrance-Vanasse; Isidro Hotzel; Yan Wu; Stephen Ra; Richard Bonneau; Kyunghyun Cho; Andreas Loukas; Vladimir Gligorijevic; Saeed Saremi. Outstanding Paper.

The paper stages its evidence from computable sequence metrics to laboratory expression and functional binding. That ordering is more instructive for our problem than the domain-specific architecture. In Figure 2 and Algorithm 1 (p. 4), a compact visual explanation is paired with a short procedure, so intuition and implementation remain adjacent. In Table 4 (p. 8), predicted binding and measured binding occupy separate columns, and unavailable measurements remain explicit. The introductory molecular renderings are identified as folded visualizations rather than laboratory observations. These choices prevent one evidence level from silently substituting for another. The appendix supplies training, profiling, prompting, and experimental protocols, with a contents list on p. 13. For our manuscript, residual suppression, modeled correction authority, and observed robot task success should be presented as similarly distinct quantities, with exact links between the claims and their corresponding measurements.

**[Vision Transformers Need Registers](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/file/0b408293619f725fd30162af057e531a-Paper-Conference.pdf)** — Timothée Darcet; Maxime Oquab; Julien Mairal; Piotr Bojanowski. Outstanding Paper.

A visual anomaly becomes the organizing question. Figure 1 (p. 1) shows attention maps before and after adding registers across several model families. The paper then measures token norms, probes the information in outlier tokens, states a hypothesis, introduces a small intervention, and checks downstream performance and register-count ablations. This sequence gives the simple modification an explanatory basis. The detailed OpenCLIP exception is instructive: Table 3 (p. 8) reports worse object-discovery scores with registers, and the body acknowledges the loss, although the caption's broad improvement wording is stronger than those rows support. We should learn from both aspects—retain cross-model failures, and make captions as qualified as the surrounding discussion. Longer diagnostics go to the appendix, while the central anomaly, intervention, and consequence remain visible within the nine-page main argument.

**[Robust agents learn causal world models](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/file/44a2b9f7bf9aec3f1fa333ad875b0ee0-Paper-Conference.pdf)** — Jonathan Richens; Tom Everitt. Honorable Mention.

The paper earns a broad conceptual claim by first defining its narrow mathematical setting. Figure 1 (p. 4) uses three related causal diagrams to expose what changes between training and testing, with node and edge meanings in the caption. Page 5 concentrates the theorem statements, but includes qualifications and an explanation of the measure-zero exclusion rather than displaying bare results. Later prose interprets necessity, sufficiency, and approximation separately; proofs are deferred to Appendices C–E. The main limitations explicitly restrict the results to unmediated decision tasks and a large intervention family. Thus this is a useful writing model, not a theorem that automatically transfers to robot control. Our authority and composite-stability results likewise need their interface assumptions beside the statement, a concrete interpretation, and a clear boundary between a proved finite-dimensional result and an unverified robot-wide guarantee.

**[Proving Test Set Contamination in Black-Box Language Models](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/file/46e624c244cff669223d488defd4e835-Paper-Conference.pdf)** — Yonatan Oren; Nicole Meister; Niladri Chatterji; Faisal Ladhak; Tatsunori Hashimoto. Honorable Mention.

The strongest structural choice is the separation of a statistical guarantee from the assumptions needed when applying it. The method begins with a simple permutation argument, then introduces a more practical sharded test and distinguishes finite-sample from asymptotic validity. Experiments first use intentional contamination as a positive control, then evaluate public models with a negative control. Around Table 2 (p. 9), the text explicitly discusses non-rejection, multiplicity, and heuristic filtering; p. 10 explains why the filtered MMLU aggregate no longer provides the same proof. This is directly relevant to our calibration and paired-outcome audit: distinguish proven test properties from the provenance of an actual run. The public proceedings PDF has substantive discussion through p. 11 and appendices from p. 16, so its current layout should not be read as evidence for an original nine-page submission format.

**[Meta Continual Learning Revisited: Implicitly Enhancing Online Hessian Approximation via Variance Reduction](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/file/0b6df1a973b82b3cf7fadca6c387ae5a-Paper-Conference.pdf)** — Yichen Wu; Long-Kai Huang; Renzhen Wang; Deyu Meng; Ying Wei. Honorable Mention.

A common mathematical representation makes the comparison legible before the new algorithm appears. Table 1 (p. 3) aligns existing approaches by Hessian approximation and update structure; Figures 1–2 then reuse visual grammar to expose the change. The experiment section is organized around six specific questions, including buffer size, imbalance, backbone choice, alternative variance-reduction methods, and the proposed mechanism itself. Figure 3 (p. 9) measures relative gradient variance instead of relying solely on accuracy tables. The detailed proofs and extensive settings move to the appendix, but the approximate nature of the main propositions is visible. For our observer comparison, a similarly explicit table can show shared calibration, covariance choices, projection, and the tracking term. However, the analogy does not establish composite adaptation as best; that ranking must remain an empirical result with uncertainty, independent of the attractive common formulation.

**What these examples imply for the adaptation manuscript.** These are editorial inferences from the named examples, not conference requirements.

- **Organize around the scientific distinction.** The most useful narrative is the difference between a calibrated observation model identifying a disturbance and an available correction actually repairing a task. A common observer representation can make the comparison clear; it cannot substitute for fair tuning or a held-out comparison. The reader should encounter the numerical counterevidence before any broad performance claim.
- **Assign a separate job to each figure.** An overview can explain the policy–controller–robot interface; a scene montage can make the task concrete; a quantitative panel can establish a success or harm finding. Authentic scenes should retain method, condition, timing, and selection qualifiers. A failure chart is preferable to using an unrelated scene as though it depicted that failure. The examples offer no universal requirement that Figure 1 contain a pipeline: it is a before/after image grid in Registers, a comparison chart in Never Train, a system montage in UniSim, and a formal scenario diagram in Robust agents.
- **Use tests that could reject the explanation.** A physical-authority counterexample, joint-dependent harm, and a healthy-condition regression test are scientifically informative when they constrain the mechanism. They should not be confined to a generic limitations paragraph. Main quantitative panels can show all-joint directions of change and uncertainty; the appendix can retain the full condition tables and telemetry provenance.
- **Make prose and captions agree about uncertainty.** “Numerically strongest” is different from “established best,” and an unresolved comparison is different from equivalence. A compact caption must still preserve which outcomes are held out, which control is paired, and whether a correction is held or updated continuously. Attractive qualitative evidence does not justify a deterministic counterfactual claim.
- **Keep decision-relevant theory in the main text.** State the finite-dimensional assumptions, the result, and its physical interpretation together. Put long algebra in the appendix with direct pointers. A bound that has not been implemented as a robot certificate must retain that distinction. The inspected theory papers are useful models for assumption placement; their control-related language is not a license to apply their theorems outside their stated settings.

**Citation and space practices.** There is no support here for a minimum citation target. Manual bibliography-entry counts for two short-reference winners are 37 for Diffusion Geometry (18 on p. 10, 19 on p. 11) and 34 for Registers (17 on each page). These count bibliography entries, not in-text citations. More useful than those totals is citation function: credit the inherited identity where it is used, cite the exact baseline protocol in experiments, and distinguish the closest prior mechanism before stating the contribution. Several examples consolidate related work late; Never Train places it after references, while Diffusion Geometry distributes it through the argument. A nine-page manuscript should therefore prioritize a complete claim–evidence chain rather than imitate one section order or compress references to manufacture room. Current public PDF length is not proof of the submission-time page limit.

The strongest transferable pattern is precision about what each piece of evidence establishes. It supports a manuscript centered on diagnosed interface limitations and tested repairs even when the extra composite term does not beat a tuned Kalman baseline.
