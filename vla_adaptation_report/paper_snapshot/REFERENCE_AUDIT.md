## Official ICLR 2027 guidance

The [2027 author guidelines](https://iclr.cc/Conferences/2027/AuthorGuidelines) are available, so a 2026 fallback is unnecessary. Initial main text is limited to **9 pages**; discussion and camera-ready versions may use 10. References are unlimited, and appendices follow references but need not be read by reviewers. The paper and supplement must be anonymous. A reproducibility statement is recommended; ethics and reproducibility statements are excluded from the main-text limit.

The [2027 call for papers](https://www.iclr.cc/Conferences/2027/CallForPapers) and author guidelines agree on **September 18, 2026, 23:59 AoE** for abstracts and **September 25, 2026, 23:59 AoE** for papers. Recheck immediately before submission. Some reviewer-FAQ text still says September 16 and contains old-year references; it should not override the current author instructions and call.

The [2027 reviewer guide](https://iclr.cc/Conferences/2027/ReviewerGuidelines) emphasizes a clear research question, sound motivation and relation to prior work, technical correctness, experimental rigor, reproducibility, and valuable new knowledge. State-of-the-art results are not required. For this paper, the defensible contribution can be the reproducible boundary of calibrated repair and estimator selection; an unsupported universal composite/Kalman ordering would weaken the evidence. Important qualifications and central outcomes belong in the main text because supplementary reading is optional. This last recommendation is our application of the criteria, not a conference requirement about this specific paper.

The [2027 AI policy for authors](https://iclr.cc/Conferences/2027/AIPolicyForAuthors) requires a disclosure section, excluded from the page limit, and disclosure in the submission form. Required categories include experiment/methodology design, method implementation, mathematical claims/proofs, hypotheses, and result interpretation. Given this workflow, a disclosure limited to proofreading or reference formatting would be incomplete. Human authors remain responsible for checking the final scientific claims. The exact statement must reflect what each collaborator actually did; this audit does not assert that a human verification step occurred when it has not.
