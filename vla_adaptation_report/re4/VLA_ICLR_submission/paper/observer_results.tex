\section{Identifiability and scope of the sampled guarantees}
\label{app:implemented-theory}
The main text states the update/hold recursions, delayed-application bound,
finite-horizon tube, and its active-every-step invariant-region specialization.
The following restriction explains the independent bias calibration they need.

\begin{proposition}[Additive ambiguity]
\label{prop:ambiguity}
In $y_k=S(u_k+f)+b_y+\varepsilon_k^{\rm obs}$, with constant $f,b_y$ and invertible $S$,
every pair $(f+v,b_y-Sv)$ produces the same observations. Without an
independent restriction on $b_y$, only $Sf+b_y$ is identifiable.
\end{proposition}
Healthy calibration or a known healthy prefix supplies a bias reference,
subject to history and modeling error. With known nonsingular sensitivity,
additive fault observation does not require new command excitation. Gain
identification has a different regressor and information requirement.
For convenience the sampled analysis uses
\begin{equation}
z_k=f_k+w_k,\qquad \|w_k\|\le\epsilon_k,\qquad
E_k=\|\fhat_k-f_k\|,\qquad \|f_{k+1}-f_k\|\le\nu_k.
\label{eq:obsbound}
\end{equation}
Bounds on $w_k$ must hold on corrected trajectories. A mean healthy residual
does not itself establish a bound in new contact modes. Likewise, the local
incremental inequality in Eq.~\eqref{eq:incremental} concerns the physical execution map under the same supplied VLA commands
(Appendix~\ref{app:history-partition}). The certificate includes necessary
low-level controller state, but does not require VLA internal memory or a
VLA Jacobian. A healthy task rollout generally supplies different commands.
The proofs and quantitative error budget follow in Appendix~\ref{app:theory}.
