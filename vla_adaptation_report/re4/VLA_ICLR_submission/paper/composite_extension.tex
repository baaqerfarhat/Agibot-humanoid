\section{Continuous composite adaptation as an optional extension}
\label{sec:composite-extension}
This appendix derives the geometric tracking term that can augment
prediction feedback when the same-command nominal execution response is
available online. The composite controller was not evaluated in the VLA
experiments; the main results instead analyze the documented sampled laws.
Appendix~\ref{sec:gen-theory} specifies physical matching, reference,
and domain assumptions, including the closure of the physical state.

\subsection{An execution metric generates the tracking signal}
Let $r(t)$ be the commands actually supplied by the frozen VLA and $\xi$
the robot and low-level controller state. The nominal physical response
$\xi^{\rm nom}$ follows that \emph{same command sequence}, without rerunning the
VLA. For $\tilde\theta=\hat\theta-\theta^*$, physical OOD and allocation give
\begin{equation}
 \dot\xi=F_{\rm exec}(\xi,r,t)-B_\theta\tilde\theta+d_{\rm eff},
 \quad \dot\xi^{\rm nom}=F_{\rm exec}(\xi^{\rm nom},r,t).
 \label{eq:gen-matching}
\end{equation}
Here $F_{\rm exec}$ contains the robot and its execution controller, and
$B_\theta=G\Psi$; allocation/model errors enter $d_{\rm eff}$
(Appendix~\ref{sec:gen-theory}). On a geodesically convex operating domain,
assume a smooth metric $M_c(\xi,t)$ independent of $r$ and $\hat\theta$,
with $0<\underline mI\preceq M_c\preceq\overline mI$, and uniformly over
admissible commands,
\begin{equation}
 \partial_tM_c+\partial_{F_{\rm exec}}M_c+
 A_{\rm exec}^\top M_c+M_cA_{\rm exec}\preceq-2\lambda M_c,
 \quad A_{\rm exec}=\left.\partial_\xi F_{\rm exec}\right|_{r\,\mathrm{fixed}},
 \quad\lambda>0.
 \label{eq:contraction-metric}
\end{equation}
No VLA Jacobian is required. Appendix~\ref{app:gen-innerloop} constructs
the metric from physical tracking dynamics and controller gains.
For a constant-speed minimizing geodesic $c$ from $\xi^{\rm nom}$ to $\xi$, set
\begin{equation}
 V_\xi=\tfrac12\int_0^1c_s^\top M_c(c,t)c_s\,ds,\quad
 X=\sqrt{2V_\xi},\quad p_\xi=M_c(\xi,t)c_s(1),\quad
 \sigma_{\rm exec}=B_\theta^\top p_\xi.
 \label{eq:contraction-energy}
\end{equation}
First variation of this half-energy (Appendix~\ref{app:first-variation})
gives
\begin{equation}
 D^+V_\xi\le-\lambda X^2-\sigma_{\rm exec}^\top\tilde\theta
                  +p_\xi^\top d_{\rm eff},\qquad
 |p_\xi^\top d_{\rm eff}|\le X\|d_{\rm eff}\|_{M_c}.
 \label{eq:gen-storage}
\end{equation}
Thus the metric generates the adaptive tracking signal; for constant
$M_c=P$, $\sigma_{\rm exec}=B_\theta^\top P(\xi-\xi^{\rm nom})$
\citep{lopez2020adaptivecontraction,tsukamoto2021adaptivecontraction}.
The primary certificate excludes VLA policy memory; physical execution
memory must satisfy the partition in Appendix~\ref{app:history-partition}.

\subsection{Composite extension and the evaluated prediction branch}
A weighted regression supplies $b_I=H_I\theta^*+\epsilon_I$.
With constant $\Gamma\succ0$, its prediction gradient and the geodesic
coupling suggest
\begin{equation}
 \dot{\hat\theta}=\operatorname{Proj}_{\Theta}^{\Gamma}
 \!\left(\hat\theta,\Gamma[\kappa\sigma_{\rm exec}+k_c(b_I-H_I\hat\theta)]\right),
 \quad k_c>0,\quad 0\le\kappa\le1.
 \label{eq:gen-law}
\end{equation}
The velocity projection uses the $\Gamma^{-1}$ metric on a fixed convex
set containing the true parameter and initial estimate. Adding
$V_\theta=\|\tilde\theta\|_{\Gamma^{-1}}^2/2$ cancels
$-\sigma_{\rm exec}^\top\tilde\theta$ exactly at $\kappa=1$.

\begin{theorem}[Contraction-derived execution recovery]
\label{thm:gen-adaptive}
For the same-command execution pair in \eqref{eq:gen-matching}, suppose
the metric, matching, and projection assumptions hold after $t_e$ without
state, metric, or parameter jumps, with absolutely continuous $\theta^*$, $H_I\succeq\mu I$, $\mu>0$,
$\|d_{\rm eff}\|_{M_c}\le\bar d_M$,
$\|\epsilon_I\|\le\bar\epsilon_I$, and
$\|\dot\theta^*\|\le\nu$. For \eqref{eq:gen-law} with $\kappa=1$, put
$\gamma_+=\lambda_{\max}(\Gamma)$ and $\gamma_-=\lambda_{\min}(\Gamma)$.
Then, while these domain assumptions hold,
\begin{align}
 W(t)&\le e^{-\beta(t-t_e)}W(t_e)+\frac Q\beta(1-e^{-\beta(t-t_e)}),
 \quad W^2=X^2+\|\tilde\theta\|_{\Gamma^{-1}}^2,
 \label{eq:gen-envelope}\\
 \beta&=\min\{\lambda,k_c\mu\gamma_-\},\qquad
 Q^2=\bar d_M^2+\left(k_c\sqrt{\gamma_+}\bar\epsilon_I+
                  \nu/\sqrt{\gamma_-}\right)^2.
 \label{eq:gen-forcing}
\end{align}
\end{theorem}
This gives $\|\xi-\xi^{\rm nom}\|\le W/\sqrt{\underline m}$ and
$\|\tilde\theta\|\le\sqrt{\gamma_+}W$, with a noise/drift floor.
This bounds physical execution relative to the supplied-command response;
it does not certify a separately evolving healthy VLA rollout or task
success. The composite extension requires an available $\xi^{\rm nom}$ and
$\sigma_{\rm exec}$; it was not evaluated in the VLA runs.

The evaluated architecture uses prediction feedback only ($\kappa=0$);
$\xi^{\rm nom}$ may then remain an analytical comparison and no online geodesic
is required. Its observation-attenuated law is a biased prediction step, not a literal
discretization of the unbiased law above. Actual normalization, holds, and
sampling are analyzed in Section~\ref{sec:discrete}. For the prediction-only continuous law,
\begin{equation}
 \begin{bmatrix}D^+X\\D^+Z\end{bmatrix}\le
 \begin{bmatrix}-a&b\\d&-c\end{bmatrix}
 \begin{bmatrix}X\\Z\end{bmatrix}+\begin{bmatrix}d_0\\q_z\end{bmatrix},
 \qquad a,c>0,\quad ac>bd,\quad Z=\|\tilde\theta\|_{\Gamma^{-1}}.
 \label{eq:prediction-comparison}
\end{equation}
Appendix~\ref{sec:prediction-comparison} derives these coefficients from
metric sensitivity, information, and predictor error. They express how
nominal contraction and identification must dominate their feedback
coupling. A stable cascade ($d=0$) permits large one-way sensitivity $b$.
A physical execution metric and its domain have since been measured for the
reported robots: established on the GR1 arm, refuted on the Panda under
operational-space control (Appendix~\ref{app:measured-certificates}). The
composite law is therefore evaluable on the humanoid arm and not on the
Panda. Full-VLA contraction is not required.
