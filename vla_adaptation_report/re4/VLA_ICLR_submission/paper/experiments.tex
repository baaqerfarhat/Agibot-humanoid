\section{Experimental evidence}
\label{sec:experiments}
\paragraph{Protocol and scope.}
All task-success results are in simulation. LIBERO \citep{libero} adapts
within episodes; ALOHA \citep{zhao2023aloha} and GR1 identify on prior
episodes. Comparisons pair task and initial state; policy sampling is not
seed-pinned, and repeated $n{=}20$ cells vary by up to $\pm2$ successes
(Appendix~\ref{app:statistics}). \emph{Fixed}/\emph{broken} count failure-to-success/success-to-failure
changes. Implementation provenance was recovered for all nine cohorts, and
the rotation mask is reproduced by a preregistered healthy-only gate
(Appendix~\ref{app:protocols}). Every comparison below was preregistered with
its refutation condition written first, and the refutations are reported as
primary.

With the shipped calibration, success increases from 23.3\% to 65.0\%
(paired gain 41.7 points; approximate 95\% interval $[32.5,50.8]$ under an
episode-iid model), all four suites significant after Bonferroni correction.
The held-out replication was registered with $\pm3$ reproduction bands:
three suites reproduce within band; the long-horizon suite is
\emph{refuted}, dropping to 7/40 against a registered threshold of five. A task-clustered bootstrap leaves the pooled effect intact under both
calibrations ($[+36,+65]$ and $[+20,+49]$ episodes), and three of four suites
individually significant at cluster level under the shipped calibration
against one of four under the held-out one.
The honest headline is therefore two numbers -- 78/120 and 68/120, or 67\%
and 58\% of matched health -- because the measured sensitivity varies with
the probed initial state by up to a factor of two in single axes
(Appendix~\ref{app:calibration}) and long-horizon episodes give that error
200+ steps to accumulate.

\subsection{Where correction works, where it fails, and the interface ceiling}

Translation offset $0.15$ improves $4/20\to19/20$, while uniform offset
$0.15$ reaches only $2/20$ with translation left uncorrected; mixed-sign
rotation improves $2/12\to10/12$. Under changing offsets correction still
improves task success despite waveform RMS errors of 45--79\% of fault
amplitude, and gain faults $g=.30$ and $.20$ both reach $17/20$, from $1/20$
and $0/20$ (Appendices~\ref{app:severity}--\ref{app:gain},
Figure~\ref{fig:map}).

The measured residual exposes a limitation beyond matrix conditioning: for
true $x$-offset $+0.05$ the separated $x$ estimate is $-0.0130$ under a
uniform six-axis fault but $+0.0244$ under a single-axis one, so an
approximately condition-$3$ healthy sensitivity does not ensure accurate
reconstruction on faulted trajectories (Appendix~\ref{app:centering}).

\paragraph{Registered baselines and the ceiling.}
On the headline fault with full logs, the method reproduces to the episode
($6\to18$ of 20 spatial, $0\to15$ of 40 long-horizon; frozen rates move with
unpinned sampling). A calibrated \emph{static} observer ($K=0$) matches it on the short suite;
its six-of-forty long-horizon gap ($p=.07$) did not survive a seed-pinned
rerun at $n=80$ ($10/80$ versus $9/80$, $p=1.0$), so FIR memory is
\emph{refuted} as a resolvable advantage on that suite. The \emph{innovation} law lands
within three episodes of the deployed law on both cohorts and removes the
attenuation bias on the well-identified rotation channels.
\emph{Known-fault correction through the same interface} reaches $19/20$
and $22/40$: the estimated correction attains 1.09 and 0.71 of its net gain. The long-horizon shortfall localizes to $r_y$, and its cause is measured:
plants fitted to the closed-loop healthy log recover an $r_y$ DC gain near
$.10$ against the probe's $.28$, and on all three rotation channels the
settled estimate tracks that ratio
(Appendix~\ref{app:measured-certificates}). With all six channels corrected exactly,
spatial reaches $20/20$ against a healthy $19/20$, so the fault is fully
cancellable through this interface and the rotation-only mask costs at most
one episode of ceiling. Against the best raw-error integral gain, run paired and seed-pinned on one
scenario set, the method wins $18/20$ to $3/20$ (15 fixed, 0 broken,
$p=6.1\cdot10^{-5}$): the integral's $x$ estimate never arrives and its $z$
overshoots (Appendices~\ref{app:gain}--\ref{app:oracle}).

\subsection{Policy reuse, new robots, and what holding buys}
The LIBERO predictor and sensitivity fitted with $\pi_{0.5}$ are reused
unchanged with OpenVLA-OFT and GR00T N1.7: on spatial rotation, translation
and gain faults, OFT reaches $11$, $14$ and $17$ of 20 from zero, and N1.7
$14$, $15$ and $19$ from $0$, $2$ and $0$ -- calibration reuse between
policies on one robot. ALOHA and GR1 receive robot-specific calibration.

A registered comparison then equalized the information budget: every arm
starts from the \emph{same} stored estimate and differs only after step~0.
On ALOHA the innovation law updating throughout repairs $14/40$ against the
held $15/40$ ($p=1.0$), while the deployed attenuation law repairs $0/40$,
its estimate pulled to $.016$ rad within twenty steps by the attenuated fixed
point and deadzone leakage -- a $0.42$ cm standing error at the grasp. Failure
under continued adaptation is that law's bias, not updating as such. On GR1
the innovation law loses five of thirty to updating ($22/30$ against
$17/30$), so holding is required where the task margin is smaller than the
update's own movement (Appendix~\ref{app:transferdetails}). Healthy
adaptation changes $+1$/$0$ net of 120 ($p=1.0$).

\paragraph{The execution certificate, measured.}
Figure~\ref{fig:servo-main} instantiates the same-command bound on a
constructed servo; on the evaluated robots the certificate was then measured,
and splits. The GR1 right arm certifies as a first-order servo (poles
$.72$--$.77$); ALOHA at 50\,Hz does not. The Panda under \code{OSC\_POSE}
certifies per-axis tracking yet has \emph{no same-command contraction}:
replaying identical commands from perturbed states gives a per-step gap ratio
of $1.00$ in all four preregistered metrics, because the controller sets each
goal relative to the current pose. Theorem~\ref{thm:tube} is therefore
finite-horizon only there -- propagated with the measured constants it covers
every step but ends near $100\times$ the largest measured error -- and the
small-gain condition fails through $a=1-\lambda$, not through the coupling
constants, which are small (Appendix~\ref{app:measured-certificates}).

\paragraph{Evidence boundary.}
These results support correction of the tested execution shifts; task
margins, arbitrary perceptual OOD, and deadlines remain uncertified. Adapter
compute measures $.17$/$.46$/$.77$ ms (median/p99/max) per control step
against a policy call near $900$ ms
(Appendix~\ref{app:identificationtraces}). Sustained recovery time is not
reported against a decay rate, the measured $\lambda\approx1$ implying none
here. X2 hardware results are sensing diagnostics; paired physical-robot
repair is unevaluated.
