# ICLR 2026 oral and award papers: corpus and writing study

Collected 2026-09-08. This is a study of presentation and scientific argument, not an independent verification of these papers' results.

## Collection scope and provenance

The directly retrieved [official oral schedule](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2026/events/oral) contains **223 oral presentation events**. The [official oral/poster JSON](https://iclr.cc/static/virtual/data/iclr-2026-orals-posters.json) identifies **224 unique OpenReview IDs accepted as oral**: the extra record is *Premise Selection for a Lean Hammer*, `m04JJNeRK6`, with an oral acceptance and [poster event](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2026/poster/10007627), but no scheduled oral event. This collection includes it and records the distinction. The earlier search-cache count of 225 is not used as the current catalog count.

The [official award announcement](https://blog.iclr.cc/2026/04/23/announcing-the-iclr-2026-outstanding-papers/) names two Outstanding Papers and one honorable mention, all already within the oral corpus. It describes a 36-paper longlist and five-paper shortlist, but names only the three recognized papers. Searches of the official public announcement and associated official pages did not identify the other two shortlisted titles. No unnamed paper is assigned a finalist or candidate designation.

**Downloaded: 224/224 PDFs; errors: 0; total: 7,250 PDF pages and 1,903,309,628 PDF bytes.** Every catalog title had exactly one normalized match in the [official 2026 proceedings](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2026). Each PDF URL comes from that paper's official proceedings abstract metadata. Files were opened with PyMuPDF and every page was text-extracted. Nine first-page titles differ from the catalog through typography or title revision; their actual printed titles were checked and retained separately in the manifest. For example, `Pinet` is printed as `Πnet`, and the GLASS Flows subtitle differs.

Version qualification: these are the public official proceedings PDFs retrieved on the collection date. OpenReview access returned an access challenge elsewhere in this collection session; no access-control workaround was attempted. The files are **not asserted to be the latest OpenReview revisions or original submission versions**. The manifest preserves the event identity, proceedings URL, printed title, file hashes, and retrieval provenance so these distinctions remain inspectable.

Artifacts:

- [Collector](collect_2026.py): at most two simultaneous requests, bounded retries/backoff, atomic downloads, PDF validation, title checks, SHA-256 receipts.
- [Manifest](manifest_2026.json): one record per real OpenReview ID, including verified designations and absolute PDF/text paths.
- [Collection metadata](metadata_2026.json): raw-source hashes, counts, version limitations, collector hash.
- Bulk directory: `/home/fengze/online_adaptation_research/iclr_2023_2026/2026/`, with `raw/`, `pdf/`, `text/`, and selected `reading_previews/`; bulk PDFs remain outside git.

Reproduction: from the repository, run `python paper/style_study/collect_2026.py`. Existing downloaded inputs are reused; delete or relocate a cached source deliberately before requesting a new catalog snapshot. The page-delimited extracted texts use one-based **PDF page numbers**, which are also the page convention below.

## Reading depth

All 224 papers received automated metadata collection, source matching, PDF validation, and complete text extraction. The nine papers below received a separate targeted close reading of their introductions, principal methods/results, relevant limitations, and appendix organization. Representative pages were rendered and visually inspected. This is not a claim that every proof or every appendix page of all 224 papers was read or validated.

The selection contains all three publicly named award recipients plus six oral papers relevant to control, robotics, adaptive optimization, or constrained methods. It deliberately spans theoretical, empirical, and systems styles; it is not a random sample for estimating how all accepted papers are written.

## Three recognized papers

### Transformers are Inherently Succinct — Outstanding

[Official PDF](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2026/file/5f7804e8855efe5554025217abc49315-Paper-Conference.pdf), 21 pages; `Yxz92UuPLQ`.

The introduction on pp. 1–2 replaces a familiar comparison criterion with a more informative one, then states the resulting separations and costs. Definitions occupy pp. 2–5 because later statements genuinely depend on them. The concrete construction on p. 6 prepares the reader for the complexity argument. On p. 9, Theorem 15 is followed by a short proof organized into named directions and three numbered construction steps; related consequences immediately reuse it. There are no figures or tables: mathematical dependency provides the visual hierarchy. Related work is concentrated in the concluding discussion on p. 10 while foundational citations also appear at their actual use sites. References occupy pp. 11–13; detailed proofs begin on p. 14. Transferable lesson: expose the main proof mechanism and assumption scope in the main text, and move repetitive construction details to the appendix. A figure quota would add little here.

### LLMs Get Lost In Multi-Turn Conversation — Outstanding

[Official PDF](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2026/file/59f6421e64707225fdf5b28840679a07-Paper-Conference.pdf), 41 pages; `VKGTGGcwl6`.

Figure 1 on p. 1 combines miniature conversations with a two-axis diagnostic, making the conceptual failure visible before the protocol is introduced. Figure 2 on p. 3 separately identifies the evaluated assistant and the surrounding simulation/evaluation components. The method then separates instruction construction, conversation execution, and comparison conditions. Table 1 and Figure 5 on p. 7 pair broad scores with a decomposition of those scores; the second display answers a different question from the first. Related work on pp. 2–3 groups evaluation choices by their assumptions and tradeoffs. The main text limits interpretation to a controlled underspecification stress test on pp. 10–11; appendices provide examples, prompts, inspection, and implementation details beginning on p. 17. Transferable lesson: a memorable finding benefits from a second diagnostic display and an explicit account of what the test does—and does not—measure.

### The Polar Express — Honorable mention

[Official PDF](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2026/file/df88b275bef31ac96c85f0c4013734fc-Paper-Conference.pdf), 37 pages; `yRtgZ1K8hO`.

The paper connects an application result early (Figure 1, p. 2) to a precise approximation objective (p. 4), then explains why the proposed construction solves it. Figure 2 on p. 5 shows how the approximation interval changes across three iterations next to the central theorem. Algorithm 1 on p. 7 color-separates offline coefficient preparation from online matrix computation; that separation communicates the practical contribution more effectively than another architecture diagram. Finite-precision changes receive their own main-text subsection on p. 7. Figure 5 on p. 10 compares approximation effort with both optimization quality and runtime. The appendix contents on p. 15 direct readers to code, related work, proofs, numerical details, and expanded experiments. Transferable lesson: distinguish ideal mathematics, the implemented numerical variant, and downstream benefit; give each its own evidence.

## Six relevant oral papers

### Differentiable Model Predictive Control on the GPU

[Official PDF](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2026/file/d66ccb30875ae43e87f6afa5640d9585-Paper-Conference.pdf), 19 pages; `bFYfV6c9zu`.

The opening specifies a computational bottleneck and the optimization problem to be accelerated. Related work precedes a short differentiable-optimization primer on pp. 2–3, allowing the method to focus on what changes. The forward and backward derivations explicitly share the same linear-system structure. Figure 2 and Algorithms 1–2 on p. 6 align the data flow with executable steps and equation references. The hardware demonstration on p. 9 uses authentic vehicle imagery beside state and trajectory plots, rather than relying on photographs as quantitative evidence. The discussion states implementation limitations, including inequality-constraint handling and approximate gradients. Additional solver details start on p. 13. Transferable lesson: connect the algebraic structure responsible for the improvement to an implementation diagram, then separate synthetic timing evidence from physical deployment evidence.

### Rodrigues Network for Learning Robot Actions

[Official PDF](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2026/file/eb9b18ccb76a1156af5779ffdca1d91f-Paper-Conference.pdf), 23 pages; `IZHk6BXBST`.

Figure 1 on p. 1, placed before the abstract, shows the transformation from a classical robot operator to a learnable operator and then to applications. Repeated robot, matrix, and feature symbols make the changed component easy to locate. Related work on pp. 2–3 distinguishes graph connectivity, attention modifications, and forward-kinematics layers by the structural information each supplies. The method builds from operator to block to full network; Figure 2 on p. 5 is a useful second level of detail. Experiments progress from controlled kinematic tests to manipulation and hand reconstruction. Table 2 on p. 9 retains per-task variation, including a task where the proposed model is not strongest; Table 3 separates reported and reproduced baseline results. Transferable lesson: organize the narrative around the component changed, and keep local limitations visible inside the main comparison.

### Mean Flow Policy with Instantaneous Velocity Constraint for One-step Action Generation

[Official PDF](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2026/file/fe7f375ef01e43f17d2c32b28a176577-Paper-Conference.pdf), 23 pages; `mIeKe74W43`.

Figure 1 on p. 1 positions the method in a performance-versus-speed plane. The method on pp. 3–6 proceeds through two concrete subproblems—how to imitate an action and how to choose the action to imitate—before introducing the additional training constraint. Theorem 1 on p. 4 annotates a lower bound as an improvement term minus fitting-error terms, making the qualification legible. Short derivations stay in the main text; longer analysis moves to Appendix A. Table 1 and the constraint ablation on p. 8 place the new component's evidence near the main comparison; p. 9 separately reports training and inference costs. Related work appears after the experiments. Transferable lesson: a composite loss or update needs an explicit role for each term and a targeted ablation, with theoretical benefits still conditional on approximation error.

### World-In-World: World Models in a Closed-Loop World

[Official PDF](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2026/file/5b4263be85820683d78675cc18d2efc7-Paper-Conference.pdf), 40 pages; `yDmb7xAfeb`.

Figure 1 on p. 1 connects heterogeneous models, one interface, several embodied tasks, and the evaluation questions. The method's order is deliberate: common planning protocol, action conversion, tasks, then model adaptation. Figure 3 on p. 3 distinguishes imagined from executed interactions. Tables on p. 7 establish outcomes; Figure 5 on p. 8 then plots success against two intermediate measures, while Figures 6–7 separately show data and inference-budget effects. The discussion on p. 10 is organized by concrete failure sources. Broader related work begins in Appendix A on PDF p. 19. Transferable lesson: interface standardization is itself part of the scientific method, and a diagnostic metric should be compared against task outcomes rather than quietly substituted for them. Correlation plots support descriptive relationships, not causal identification by themselves.

### Task-free Adaptive Meta Black-box Optimization

[Official PDF](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2026/file/899511e37a8e01e1bd6f6f1d377cc250-Paper-Conference.pdf), 31 pages; `AufVSUgMUo`.

Figure 1 on p. 2 isolates the information-source difference from conventional meta-optimization. Figure 2 on p. 4 gives an outer adaptation loop above three aligned operator modules; subsequent equations reuse those module names. Experiments are organized around stated research questions for performance, mechanism visualization, ablation, and parameter sensitivity. Figure 5 on p. 9 retains individual problem rows in hyperparameter heatmaps, showing heterogeneity that an aggregate tuning curve could hide. The appendix starts on p. 14 with reproducibility, algorithm pseudocode, and convergence assumptions; additional baseline settings and sensitivity analyses follow. Transferable lesson: show where adaptation data originate, then distinguish evidence for the whole system from evidence about its internal operators. The dense module diagram is informative at full size but would need simplification for a compact overview figure.

### Πnet: Optimizing Hard-Constrained Neural Networks with Orthogonal Projection Layers

[Official PDF](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2026/file/66ba03db7e50b5db5593fe759527a74f-Paper-Conference.pdf), 47 pages; `EJ680UQeZG`.

Figure 1 on p. 1 identifies raw output, projection, and loss with a small geometric illustration. Related work is integrated into the introduction through constraint-handling alternatives, leading to the exact problem and contribution list. The method distinguishes forward computation from backward differentiation. Page 5 explicitly explains what finite projection iterations guarantee, qualifying the stronger idealized description. A short numerical-details subsection on p. 6 points to a substantial appendix on conditioning and tuning beginning on p. 33. Figure 3 on p. 8 plots objective quality and constraint violation separately against time, with seed variation defined in the caption. Transferable lesson: numerical conditioning can be central to the contribution; distinguish satisfying constraints from optimizing the task objective, and state the finite-iteration qualification near the algorithm.

## Citation and appendix practices

These examples support **purposeful density**, not a target reference count. Citations in related work group competing approaches by the assumption, representation, or computational tradeoff that matters to the new contribution. Citations in methods identify the inherited component precisely. Pure theory uses citations inside the proof chain; systems papers also cite solver implementations and numerical approximations.

Two bibliography counts were checked directly from the PDF entry starts, rather than from in-text citation regexes: *Transformers are Inherently Succinct* has **44 entries** on pp. 11–13 (16 + 22 + 6); *Differentiable MPC on the GPU* has **46** on pp. 10–12 (16 + 18 + 12). Entry starts were selected at the bibliography's 108-point left margin and inspected as author/title starts, excluding running headers and acknowledgments. These two observations do not define an ICLR quota.

The main/appendix boundary follows argumentative importance. Main text retains the central assumptions, key mathematical step, evaluated intervention, primary comparisons, and limitations affecting interpretation. Appendices supply longer proofs, algorithms that would interrupt the narrative, full implementation and tuning details, additional benchmarks, and qualitative examples. Several papers provide a contents page because their appendix is substantially longer than the main argument. The retrieved PDF length is therefore not a measure of main-text length.

## Implications for the interface-adaptation manuscript

These are editorial recommendations inferred from the selected examples, not new empirical findings:

1. Let the opening figure identify the frozen policy, available correction interface, disturbance locations, and observed outcomes. Keep the operator-level detail in a second figure or the method. Authentic robot imagery illustrates the setting; plots establish performance.
2. Make the argument progress from residual information, to correction authority, to closed-loop behavior. Each stage should identify its assumption and the observation that would test it.
3. Explain inherited components with direct citations and explain the new integration in concrete terms. A dense related-work paragraph should tell the reader why a comparison is relevant, rather than accumulate method names.
4. Give the composite update a role-based explanation and retain the zero-tracking comparison. Keep prediction accuracy, tracking error, and task success as distinct outcomes. A numerical ranking cannot replace the uncertainty in the comparison.
5. Put the executed update, timing, gate/normalization semantics, and principal qualification in the main paper. Put the full candidate grid, source-bound calibration details, numerical safeguards, rejected candidates, and extended proofs in a clearly indexed appendix.
6. Preserve per-joint or per-condition outcomes where an aggregate conceals harm. Use a targeted mechanism plot alongside the performance comparison; do not claim that the mechanism has been causally established by correlation alone.

The study does not imply that these stylistic choices caused oral or award selection, that every selected paper uses all of them, or that additional visual or citation density would improve the manuscript by itself.
